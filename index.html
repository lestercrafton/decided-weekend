            How it decides (so nobody has to)

            1. Everyone talks, nobody votes

            Each person answers out loud in their own words. No poll, no shortlist to rank — ranking is exactly the homework nobody wants. Rambling is fine; that's the input the decider is built for.

            2. It finds the wish underneath

            “Korean BBQ” and “dumplings” are both really saying shared plates, grilled things, order too much. The planner works at that level — cravings, energy, budget, weather-proofing — not keyword bingo.

            3. Bridges beat winners

            When wishes pull apart, it looks for the third thing that honours both: Chinese + Korean cravings became a Japanese izakaya night in the example above. Everyone can point at the plan and find their wish inside it.

            4. One plan, with receipts

            You get one itinerary, the map, the walking order, and a plain-language “why” that quotes each friend. React with I'm in, not for me, or swap a single stop — never “so… what does everyone want to do?” again.

            The little rules that make it work

            • How many is enough? Three friends checked in unlocks planning. Fewer than that and you're just planning for whoever shouted first.
            • Host can always call it. The person who started the crew can tap “Plan it for us” early — late friends' wishes fold into the next plan.
            • A soft deadline, not a hard one. Wishes close Thursday 8 p.m. for a Saturday plan (the demo shows your live countdown). Stragglers get a nudge, not a lockout.
            • Wild cards stay wild. “Unrealistic” wishes (Toronto Islands at sunset, a last-minute concert) are kept as stretch picks — the planner books the sure thing and flags the dream if tickets or weather line up.
            • Nobody is the villain. The app made the call, so nobody has to defend a choice — or apologize for hating one. “Not for me” quietly re-plans around you.

            How the real app gets built

            This page is the clickable sketch. The real version keeps the exact same screens — what changes is what's behind them:

            Accounts & crews

            Phone-number or magic-link sign-in (no passwords), one crew = one link. Friends tap the link, land mid-conversation, and talk. Crew size, quorum (default 3), and the wish deadline are set by the host at creation.

            Voice in, wishes out

            On-device speech-to-text where the phone offers it, server transcription as backup. Each wish is stored as audio + transcript + the handful of signals pulled from it: cuisines, energy, budget, indoor/outdoor, “dream” flag.

            The decider

            A planner that scores real, currently-open Toronto places (hours, distance between stops, price, group size, weather) against the crew's combined signals — then writes the “why” from the actual wishes, quoting people back to themselves.

            Booking & crew deals

            Deep links and reservation partners hold the table; for crews of 6+ the app sends the group-deal ask (set menu, off-peak seating, split bills) and reports back what the venue said — confirmed in the demo above as a draft you approve before anything is sent.

            Toronto is the test city because the demo data above is hand-checked. City two is a data job, not a rewrite: the planner runs on places + wishes, not on Toronto-specific code.
